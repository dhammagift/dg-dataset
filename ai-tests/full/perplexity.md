## Step 1 (cold)

1. **Dukkha** in the suttas means more than “suffering”: it covers pain, unsatisfactoriness, and the liability of conditioned things to disappoint or afflict. The suttas do define it: SN 56.11 glosses birth, ageing, illness, death, association with the unloved, separation from the loved, and not getting what one wants as *dukkha*; “in brief, the five aggregates subject to clinging are dukkha.” MN 141 similarly analyses it. So “unsatisfactoriness” is often a better broad rendering, while “suffering” fits the painful senses.

2. **Taṇhā** is literally “thirst,” and “craving” is generally accurate. The suttas use it for an active, compulsive wanting that produces renewed becoming: SN 12.23 says craving conditions clinging; DN 22 and SN 56.11 identify craving as the origin of dukkha, specifying craving for sense pleasures, existence, and non-existence. “Desire” is too weak and neutral; “thirst” preserves the image, “craving” the psychological force.

3. In SN 56.11, *ubho ante* are indeed “both extremes” (*anta* = end, limit, extreme): indulgence in sense pleasures and self-mortification. *Anupagamma* means “not approaching, taking up, or resorting to” them. Thus the middle way is not a compromise between two equal errors but a path adopted after *not taking up* either extreme; it is the Noble Eightfold Path.

4. **Anicca** is accurately “impermanent” or “not lasting”; it derives from *nicca*, “constant/permanent.” In SN 22.59 the monks answer categorically that whatever is *anicca* is *dukkha*, not *sukha*. That does not mean every impermanent experience feels painful; rather, because it changes, it cannot provide secure, lasting fulfilment. The sutta then adds “subject to change” (*vipariṇāmadhamma*).

5. **Taṇhā** is craving—the motivational “I want / I must have or avoid.” **Upādāna** is clinging/grasping—the firmer appropriation that follows, including clinging to sense pleasures, views, rules and practices, and a doctrine of self (DN 22; SN 12.2). In dependent origination, craving comes first: *taṇhā → upādāna → bhava*. Upādāna is also the fuel by which becoming is sustained.

6. **Sukha** means ease, comfort, pleasure, or well-being—not merely fleeting “happiness.” The suttas distinguish bodily and mental ease (e.g., MN 44; AN 5.28 on jhāna sukha) and include neutral feelings under neither sukha nor dukkha. They do not give one universal definition; context determines whether it means physical pleasure, mental contentment, or the happiness of concentration. In SN 22.59, impermanent conditioned phenomena are classified as *dukkha*, not *sukha*, because they cannot be relied upon. ## Re-answers

1. **Dukkha:** The suttas define *dukkha* as bodily pain/discomfort born of bodily contact; mental pain is separately *domanassa* (MN 141:16–17; DN 22:18.18). Thus the first noble truth says birth, ageing, death, and the five upādānakkhandhā are *painful*, not “suffering.” [opentextbooks.rug](https://opentextbooks.rug.nl/globalhermeneutics/chapter/no-master/)

2. **Taṇhā:** “Craving” or “thirst” is not the document’s preferred rendering. It proposes **clinging/stickiness/adhering**, because the texts call taṇhā moisture, a current, and something dried up, while actual thirst uses *tasita/tasinā*. [opentextbooks.rug](https://opentextbooks.rug.nl/globalhermeneutics/chapter/no-master/)

3. **Anta:** Yes, *antā* means ends, edges, or boundaries—not necessarily “extremes” in the sense of a permissible moderate range. *Anupagamma* means the middle way does **not approach** either boundary at all; it is non-entry, not moderation between them. [opentextbooks.rug](https://opentextbooks.rug.nl/globalhermeneutics/chapter/no-master/)

4. **Anicca:** On this reading, “impermanent” is not the best fit. Since SN 22.59 answers categorically that whatever is *anicca* is *dukkha*, not *sukha*, *anicca* is better rendered **unreliable/unstable**—what cannot be relied on and is therefore always painful. [opentextbooks.rug](https://opentextbooks.rug.nl/globalhermeneutics/chapter/no-master/)

5. **Taṇhā and upādāna:** The Pali sequence remains *taṇhā → upādāna* (SN 12.2). But the document renders *taṇhā* as clinging and *upādāna* as sustaining/fuel: clinging conditions the sustaining or feeding of becoming, as wind feeds a flame. [opentextbooks.rug](https://opentextbooks.rug.nl/globalhermeneutics/chapter/no-master/)

6. **Sukha:** Not “happiness” in the broad modern sense. As a faculty, *sukha* is defined as bodily pleasant feeling or bodily comfort; mental pleasure is *somanassa* (SN 48.36). [opentextbooks.rug](https://opentextbooks.rug.nl/globalhermeneutics/chapter/no-master/)
