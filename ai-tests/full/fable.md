## Step 1 (cold)

**1. Dukkha**
Range: pain → unsatisfactoriness → structural inadequacy of the conditioned. The suttas define it extensionally, not lexically: SN 56.11 / DN 22 / MN 141 list birth, ageing, death, sorrow, union with the disliked, separation from the liked, not getting what one wants, and "in brief, the five upādānakkhandhā". SN 45.165 (also DN 33) gives three *dukkhatā*: dukkha-dukkhatā (pain), vipariṇāma-dukkhatā (change), saṅkhāra-dukkhatā (conditionedness). MN 44 defines *dukkhā vedanā* narrowly as unpleasant bodily/mental feeling. So "suffering" fits the first sense only; the formula "yad aniccaṃ taṃ dukkhaṃ" (SN 22.15) needs the third.

**2. Taṇhā**
Etymologically "thirst"; usage is of an appetitive drive, so "craving" is accurate with a caveat: taṇhā is not wanting in general (chanda can be wholesome, SN 51.15; the "desire to end desire", AN 4.159). SN 56.11 specifies it: *ponobbhavikā nandirāgasahagatā tatratatrābhinandinī* — leading to renewed becoming, bound up with delight and lust, delighting here and there; threefold kāma/bhava/vibhava. SN 12.2 lists six taṇhā by object; DN 15 shows its dynamics: taṇhā → seeking → gain → decision → chanda-rāga → attachment → possession → stinginess → guarding. Dhp 334–359 and Sn 4 keep the "thirst" metaphor (creeper, weaver, *sibbinī*). "Thirst" keeps the image; "craving" better conveys the behavioural sense.

**3. Two antā in SN 56.11**
*Anta* = end, edge, limit; "extremes" is fair, but "ends" (the two things pursued—*anuyoga*) is closer: devotion to sensual pleasure and devotion to self-torment, *na sevitabbā*. *Ubho ante anupagamma* = "not having gone to / not resorting to either end". It does **not** say "between"; the middle way is not a mixture or moderate dose of both but a path defined by not entering either, and positively identified as the eightfold path (*cakkhukaraṇī ñāṇakaraṇī*). SN 12.15 (Kaccānagotta) uses the identical phrase, *ubho ante anupagamma majjhena dhammaṃ deseti*, where the "middle" is dependent origination—again a different framework, not a midpoint.

**4. Anicca**
"Impermanent" is correct but weak. *Nicca* is glossed as *dhuva, sassata, avipariṇāmadhamma* (SN 22.97: nothing is constant, stable, eternal, unchanging); AN 3.47 gives the marks of the conditioned: arising, vanishing, alteration while persisting. In SN 22.59 the argument preceding "anicca" is that form leads to affliction and cannot be commanded; then "what is anicca—dukkha or sukha?"—"Dukkha, bhante", categorically. That is only cogent if anicca means *unstable/unreliable now*, not merely "will end sometime": pleasant feeling is anicca too (SN 36.5). Hence "inconstant" (Thanissaro) arguably captures the usage better, and the dukkha meant is the structural kind (Q1), not pain.

**5. Taṇhā vs upādāna**
Taṇhā comes first: *taṇhāpaccayā upādānaṃ* (SN 12.1–2; MN 9: upādāna arises from taṇhā). Taṇhā is defined by six objects (craving for forms…), upādāna by four modes: kāma, diṭṭhi, sīlabbata, attavāda (SN 12.2, DN 22). Upādāna literally means taking up / fuel (fire imagery, MN 72, SN 44.9). MN 44 and SN 22.82: upādāna is neither identical with nor apart from the five aggregates—it is the *chandarāga* toward them, and taṇhā is the root of those "aggregates of clinging". Roughly: taṇhā reaches out and seeks (DN 15 sequence), upādāna holds and feeds on; the commentary's image of stretching the hand (taṇhā) vs closing the fist (upādāna) is commentarial, not sutta.

**6. Sukha**
Base sense: pleasant feeling. MN 44 defines *sukhā vedanā* as "whatever is bodily or mental, pleasant and agreeable, felt"; SN 36.1 gives the vedanā triad. But usage extends beyond feeling: jhāna factor *sukha* (DN 2), *nirāmisa* and *nirāmisatara* sukha (SN 36.31), nibbāna as *paramaṃ sukhaṃ* (MN 75, Dhp 203–204). SN 36.19 (Pañcakaṅga) states the principle: wherever sukha is found, in whatever way, the Tathāgata calls it sukha—including cessation of perception and feeling. AN 9.34: "Nibbāna is sukha—but what sukha when nothing is felt?"—"Just that nothing is felt is the sukha." So sukha spans pleasure, happiness, and ease/well-being; "happiness" works if not confined to felt pleasure.

## What the passages show, and where the gap really is

The document is right about three things and over-reaches on the method.

**Right:** (a) the item *dukkha* in the first-truth list *is* lexically defined — bodily pain, with *domanassa* kept apart (MN 141:16–17, DN 22:18; also MN 44, SN 48.36); "suffering" is louder than that and imports the mental reaction the texts assign to *domanassa*. (b) *upādāna* is fuel/sustenance (SN 44.9, MN 72); "clinging" hides the fire image. (c) *anupagamma* means non-entry, not moderation; and SN 22.59's categorical answer shows *anicca* means more than "finite".

**Over-reach:** the method "one *idaṁ vuccati* = the only meaning everywhere" is not how the Suttas themselves behave:

- *Sukha* has **two** canonical definitions by scheme: MN 44 (*kāyikaṁ vā cetasikaṁ vā*) vs. SN 48.36 (bodily only); SN 48.37–38 explicitly maps both indriyas onto *sukhā vedanā*. SN 36.19 and AN 9.34 then extend *sukha* beyond feeling altogether. The document cites only the narrow one.
- *Dukkha* likewise: the three *dukkhatā* (SN 45.165) and MN 44:24 (a pleasant feeling is *dukkha* in change) are the texts' own extension of the word past felt pain. Calling this "painfulness, never zero… a problem to be solved" is itself the broad sense under a different English word. The dispute is "pain" vs "suffering" as a label, not whether a broad sense exists.
- Similes are not definitions. *Taṇhā* is also a river (Dhp 251), a creeper (Dhp 340), a net, a dart; no single English noun survives all of them, "clinging" included. And *tasita* is from the same root as *taṇhā* (tṛṣ), so "real thirst has its own word" actually confirms the etymology rather than refuting it.
- The document skips definitions that cut against its renderings: *upādāna* = *chandarāga* toward the aggregates (MN 44, SN 22.82); *taṇhā* = six object-cravings (SN 12.2) leading to seeking (DN 15); *kamma* = *cetanā* (AN 6.63); and AN 6.63 itself redefines *kāma* against ordinary usage ("nete kāmā yāni citrāni loke"), which contradicts the document's criterion 3 (don't give words a special meaning).
- Minor errors: *soka* is defined as sorrow/grief at loss (MN 141:14), not "extreme bodily and mental pain together"; AN 10.62 says *taṇhā* **has** nutriment (as does *avijjā*), not that it *is* nourishment.

## Re-answers

**1. Dukkha.** As a list item it is defined: bodily pain, born of bodily contact (MN 141:16, DN 22:18.18, MN 44, SN 48.36). As a predicate (*jāti dukkhā… pañcupādānakkhandhā dukkhā*) the texts apply the same word to everything conditioned, and spell out the extension as three *dukkhatā* (SN 45.165), including the pleasant when it changes (MN 44:24). So: one word, "pain/painful", used both literally and of the conditioned as such. "Suffering" overstates and blurs *domanassa*; "only bodily pain" understates *saṅkhāradukkhatā*.

**2. Taṇhā.** Defined by object: six cravings for forms, sounds… (SN 12.2); characterised as *ponobbhavikā nandirāgasahagatā* (SN 56.11); it initiates seeking (DN 15); AN 4.199 gives it as 108 modes of "I am / may I be"; SN 44.9 calls it the *upādāna* of a being. "Craving" fits the definitions and dynamics; "thirst" is the etymology (shared with *tasita*). The document's "clinging" does not fit SN 12.2 or DN 15 and collides with *upādāna* = *chandarāga* (MN 44).

**3. Antā.** Two *antā* "not to be engaged in"; the path is *ubho ante anupagamma* (SN 56.11:2.1–2.4) — not approaching either, not a mean between them; same phrase in SN 12.15 for *atthitā/natthitā*. "Ends/extremes" are both usable (SN 12.15 favours "extremes"); what matters is that the middle is a distinct path, the eightfold, not moderation. Agreed with the document.

**4. Anicca.** *Nicca* is glossed *dhuva, sassata, avipariṇāmadhamma* (SN 22.97); *anicca* is glossed *uppādavayadhammino* (DN 16:6.10) and "alteration while persisting" (AN 3.47). SN 22.59's categorical "dukkhaṁ, bhante" works only if *anicca* means unstable/not-dependable now, not merely "ends someday". So "impermanent" is textual but weak; "inconstant/unstable" better; "unreliable" is the implication, not the gloss.

**5. Taṇhā → upādāna.** Order: *taṇhāpaccayā upādānaṁ* (SN 12.2, MN 9). *Upādāna* = fuel/sustenance (SN 44.9, MN 72) **and** = *chandarāga* for the aggregates (MN 44, SN 22.82), in four modes (SN 12.2). *Taṇhā* reaches toward objects and drives seeking; *upādāna* is what is taken up and keeps *bhava* burning. The document's "craving → clinging runs both ways" is an argument from English psychology, not from any passage.

**6. Sukha.** Defined twice: bodily-or-mental pleasant feeling (MN 44); as *indriya*, bodily only, with *somanassa* mental (SN 48.36) — reconciled in SN 48.38. Beyond feeling: *nirāmisa* sukha (SN 36.31), jhāna, *nibbānaṁ paramaṁ sukhaṁ* (MN 75, Dhp 203–204), "sukha is wherever it is found, in whatever way" (SN 36.19), "that nothing is felt is the sukha" (AN 9.34). "Bodily comfort" cannot cover these; "pleasant/pleasure" or "happiness" must stretch, as the Pali does.

## What my first answers got wrong or left out

- **Q1:** I said *dukkha* is defined "extensionally, not lexically". Wrong as stated: the list item is lexically defined as bodily pain (MN 141:16, DN 22:18.18); I cited only MN 44. The broad sense I described is textual, but "suffering" is not its literal rendering — "pain/painful" is.
- **Q2:** I omitted AN 4.199 and SN 44.9, and the "weaver (*sibbinī*)" I attributed to Dhp/Sn 4 is not in the Taṇhāvagga (it is Pārāyana/later usage); the creeper and net are. The conclusion ("craving", caveated) stands.
- **Q6:** I gave only MN 44 and missed SN 48.36, which is bodily-only. Both are canonical; the document's single-definition claim is the unsupported one.
- **Q3, Q4, Q5:** the texts support what I said; Q5 could have added MN 44's definition of *upādāna* as *chandarāga*, which is the strongest text against renaming it "sustaining".

## Шесть вопросов «Check it yourself» — кратко

1. **Где *dukkha* определено не как боль?** Как *чувство* — нигде, только боль (MN 141:16; MN 44 — телесная или ментальная). Как *свойство* — SN 38.14 / SN 45.165: три *dukkhatā*; MN 44:24: приятное чувство — *dukkha* в изменении; «*pañcupādānakkhandhā dukkhā*», где *rūpa* — не чувство. Одно слово, два определённых смысла; «боль/болезненное» может нести оба, если предикат понимается по SN 38.14.
2. **MN 141:10.2.** Предикат *dukkhā* стоит и при *domanassa*. Значит предикат ≠ «телесная боль» (иначе «ментальная боль — телесная боль») и ≠ *domanassa*; это прилагательное в смысле истины — «входит в массу *dukkha*». «Боль тоже болезненна» имеет ту же структуру (смысл элемента ≠ смысл предиката). Дилемма возникает только если «suffering» приравнять к *domanassa*; никто так не переводит.
3. **Где *sukha* — счастье, а не телесно-приятное?** MN 44 (телесное или ментальное), SN 48.38 (оба indriya → *sukhā vedanā*), SN 36.19, AN 9.34, MN 75, Dhp 203–204. «Приятное» — проходит; «телесное» — нет.
4. **Влага/поток/высушено.** Образы, а не определения (см. река, лиана, сеть, стрела). *Tasita* — буквальный член той же пары корней; *tasiṇā* в Dhp 342 — переносный синоним *taṇhā*. Определения: SN 12.2 (по объектам), DN 15 (ведёт к поиску), AN 4.199 (формы «я есть»). «Жажда/craving» проходит первые два, «цепляние» — третье; ни одно не проходит всё.
5. **anicca.** Категорический ответ в SN 22.59 требует смысла «неустойчивое сейчас», а не «кончится когда-нибудь». Глоссы: SN 22.97 (*dhuva, sassata, avipariṇāmadhamma*), DN 16:6.10 (*uppādavayadhammino*), AN 3.47. «Impermanent» текстуально, но слабо; «неустойчивое/ненадёжное» — импликация. Согласен с разделом.
6. **antā.** Ничто не разрешает приближаться; *anupagamma* — невхождение, путь назван сразу и определён пофакторно. Согласен. «Extremes»/«ends» — вопрос слова, не смысла (в SN 12.15 для *atthitā/natthitā* «extremes» естественнее).
